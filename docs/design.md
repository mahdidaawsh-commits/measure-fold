# Consensus and threat boundaries

A deterministic smart contract can compute a median once it has comparable numbers. It cannot safely interpret heterogeneous natural-language source reports by trusting one caller's extracted value. MeasureFold locates consensus at the extraction boundary: leader and validators independently acquire exact SHA-256-matched full text and judge whether the text contains one observed daily rainfall total for the configured station and target date. The validator prompt checks each proposed number, unit, quote, and `MISSING` decision, including decoy stations, forecasts, other dates, and competing totals. A valid quote's mere presence is insufficient. All source decisions must be accepted before they affect the median.

The contract normalizes decimals with integer arithmetic; no floating-point or confidence tolerance can cross a result threshold. The lower-median seed and fixed outlier rule are deterministic. `READY` requires at least three inliers, while `INSUFFICIENT` has no median. Each round stores the full source manifest, semantic report, counts, excluded source indexes, result, and hash root. A later date appends another round rather than modifying earlier results. Inputs are bounded at five reports of 5,000 bytes each and twelve rounds.

The configured repository is an authority boundary, not a guarantee of physical truth or independent ownership. The synthetic fixtures demonstrate parsing and aggregation only. The source authors can still lie, coordinate, or omit readings. Distinct IDs do not establish independent sensors. Commit-pinned URLs prevent mutable text from silently changing after a round; source hashes ensure leader and validators read the same bytes. Availability failures and validator disagreements roll back. There is no payment, custody, automatic sampling, appeals mechanism, or assertion of meteorological accuracy.
